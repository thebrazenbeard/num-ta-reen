Nûm’ta’reen Reference Grammar v2.0
The language of: Ech’ta’reen (species endonym)
Name of communication mode: Nûm’ta’reen (resonance-based state-sharing)
Home-realm endonym: Tha’veyl’ruun (Ech’ta’reen term for the NID label “Zeta-775”)
Changelog
v1.0 (2025-12-31): Initial reference grammar scaffold: Parts I–II (phonology + morphology). Corpus, syntax, semantics, dictionary, and fieldwork artifacts pending in later installments.
Method & assumptions
This grammar is written as if compiled by xenolinguistic field teams working at veil-stabilized contact sites with partial translator-array support. The analysis treats Nûm’ta’reen as a linguistic system (rule-governed mapping from signals to meanings) realized through multimodal resonance and timing.
Assumptions (explicit, consistent):
Segmentability: Despite being resonance-native, Ech’ta’reen messages can be segmented into discrete phonological words in the transliteration layer, validated by recurrent timing patterns and distributional constraints.
Phonemic stop: The apostrophe (’) in field transliteration corresponds to a phonemic laryngeal interruption (primary realization /ʔ/), not punctuation.
Echo-status grammar: Temporal reference is not encoded by tense; instead, Ech’ta’reen grammaticize echo-status (witnessed, verified across echoes, looped, branched, collapsed).
Role marking via clitics: Argument roles are encoded primarily through clitics/particles rather than inflectional case.
Register stratification: Conclave formal, field clipped, ritual register, and Anchor creole are treated as systematic varieties with predictable phonological and morphological differences.
Non-transparency constraint: Native lexical material is non-transparent to Common; any English-looking substrings in earlier drafts are excluded from the native lexicon.
Note: Later Parts (III–VII) will add a 30-utterance annotated corpus (IGT) that serves as evidence for each rule stated here.
PART I — Phonetics & phonology
I.1 Phonetic channel and “speech” abstraction
Ech’ta’reen produce communicative signals primarily through resonance modulation and timing discontinuities. For fieldwork purposes, the system is rendered in a speakable transliteration that maps stable contrasts to segmental phonemes. The transliteration is not assumed to be the native physical substrate; it is an analytic layer that preserves:
contrasts in laryngeal interruption (the phonemic stop),
contrasts in place/manner that correlate with resonance bands,
contrasts in vowel quality and length that correlate with coherence intensity,
contrasts in prosodic timing that correlate with scope and evidentiality.
I.2 Phoneme inventory (IPA)
Consonants
¹ /q/ is restricted and associated with ritual register and taboo-adjacent lexemes.
² /ʔ/ is phonemic and corresponds to orthographic apostrophe (’).
Vowels
Field teams also report a lowered [ɑ] allophone of /a/ in emphatic or strained states; this is treated as contextual rather than phonemic.
I.3 Allophony and phonetic notes
/ʔ/ is realized as [ʔ] in careful speech, [ʔ̞] (creaky interruption) intervocalically in rapid field register, and may coarticulate with uvularization near /q/.
/ɾ/ ~ /r/: [ɾ] occurs intervocalically and in fast speech; [r] (trill or strong tap) surfaces in ritual register and under emphasis.
/x/ ranges from [x] to [χ] near back vowels and in proximity to /q/.
Vowel length correlates with coherence intensity and scope marking. Long vowels are phonemic in the transliteration layer.
I.4 Syllable structure & phonotactics
Syllable template
(C)(C)V(ː)(C)
Onsets: any single consonant; clusters limited to /ʃr, xr, kw, sw/ in Conclave formal. Field clipped reduces clusters.
Codas permitted: /n ŋ k t ʃ x/ (with register-conditioned variation).
/ʔ/ does not appear as a coda in the transliteration analysis; it is boundary-linked (see below).
Apostrophe placement constraint
The phonemic stop /ʔ/ occurs:
Morphological boundaries (root+root; root+clitic in specific environments).
Vowel hiatus prevention (V’V sequences).
Caution marking: a boundary click placed before taboo-adjacent material in field speech.
I.5 Prosody: stress, timing, and scope
Stress
Default word stress is initial:
/ˈCV…/ unless vowel length attracts stress.
Phrase-level timing
Ech’ta’reen prosody encodes scope in timing, represented in transliteration by:
lengthening of a nucleus (Vː) for wide-scope echo-status;
compressed inter-word intervals for “already-collapsed” states;
double /ʔ/ (written as …’…’…) as a caution delimiter in ritualized warnings.

PART II — Morphology
II.1 Morphological typology
Ech’ta’reen in transliteration is best analyzed as root-and-particle with:
compounding (root+root, often with /ʔ/ boundary),
clitic role marking (argument roles, consent),
derivational prefixes/suffixes (register, inversion, collectivization),
minimal inflection; echo-status is treated as particles/clitics rather than tense.
II.2 Lexical categories (field analysis)
Because Nûm’ta’reen is state-forward, many roots are category-flexible.
Field categories used in this grammar:
STATE: predicative roots encoding conditions (e.g., nûm).
ACT: eventive roots (e.g., kesh).
ENTITY: stable referents (e.g., threnn “communal record-body”).
ECHO: echo-status operators (e.g., ech, reen, qir).
CLITIC: role/consent markers attaching to hosts.
II.3 Core roots (non-exhaustive seed set)
These are native roots used as productive bases.
ech (ECHO/STATE): witnessed echo channel
reen (ECHO/STATE): coherence verified across echoes
qir (ECHO/ACT): recurrence/loop operator
kesh (ACT): cross/transition
veyl (STATE/ENTITY): boundary interface
nûm (STATE/ACT): cohere/align
threnn (ENTITY/ACT): communal record-body; record merge
rhel (STATE/ACT): restore balance/coherence
vahl (STATE/ACT): latch/stabilize
keth (STATE): cross-echo ethical evaluation
II.4 Productive clitics (role marking)
Clitics attach to the right edge of the host (orthographically with “=” for analysis).
=ka AGT (agent/doer)
=en PAT (patient/undergoer)
=ru SRC (source/origin)
=va GOAL (goal/target)
=shi INST (instrument/means)
=uth CONS (consent/permission)
Example frames (schematic):
STATE X=ka Y=en ECHO
ACT X=ka Y=va ECHO
II.5 Echo-status particles (grammatical operators)
Echo-status markers occur clause-finally in the default analysis, but may cliticize to the predicate in Conclave formal.
ech EVID.WIT (directly witnessed)
reen EVID.VRF (verified across echoes)
qir LOOP (looped/recurring)
ruun RET (return-pattern asserted)
na’vyr CLP (collapse/“never-was” adjacency; taboo-adjacent)
II.6 Derivational morphology
Prefixes
vael- INV (inverse/misaligned state)
uth’- SOFT/CONS (consent-primed; reduces coercive reading)
sa’- COMF (comfort/low-force register)
na’- ABS/CLP (absence/collapse adjacency; restricted)
Suffixes
-een COLL (collective/coherent-set)
-ren INSTN (single instance/one-thread referent)
-vahl LCH (latched/stabilized state; may be laudatory or condemnatory depending on CONS)
-sekh THR (threshold/negotiable boundary state)
Compounding rule
Root₁(’)Root₂ forms a single lexical item with right-headed semantics unless specified.
/ʔ/ boundary is obligatory if Root₁ ends in a vowel and Root₂ begins in a vowel; optional elsewhere but used to mark semantic boundary or taboo caution.
II.7 Worked derivations
Ech’ta’reen (endonym)
Structure: ech + ’ + ta + ’ + reen
Analysis: ECHO.WIT + BND + DEM(2nd-person salience marker) + BND + ECHO.COH
Interpretation: “those whose identity is coherent echo-address” (self-name; outsider transliterations lose the stops).
Nûm’ta’reen (communication mode)
Structure: nûm + ’ + ta + ’ + reen
Interpretation: “coherence-state-sharing” (not ‘speech’)
Veyl’sekh (threshold state)
Structure: veyl + ’ + sekh
Interpretation: boundary in threshold/negotiable state.
Uth’nûm (consent-primed alignment)
Structure: uth’ + nûm
Interpretation: alignment offered as permission-request or non-coercive approach.
Vael’kesh’vahl (misaligned crossing that latched)
Structure: vael- + kesh + ’ + vahl
Interpretation: crossing resulting in trap/lock (often used for phase-locked Anchors).
II.8 Register-conditioned morphology (overview)
Conclave formal: full echo particles, permitted onset clusters, conservative /ʔ/ boundary marking.
Field clipped: vowel shortening, reduced clusters, greater reliance on final echo markers; frequent pragmatic /ʔ/ as caution.
Ritual register: increased /q/ usage, stronger [r], longer vowels; taboo lexemes licensed.
Anchor creole: loanword integration (Common nouns), expansion of pronouns, adoption of linear tense markers in contact situations (treated as calques, not native grammar).
PART III — Syntax
III.1 Clause architecture: “state packet” model
The default Ech’ta’reen clause is best described as a state packet: a predicate (STATE/ACT) followed by participants marked for role, then a clause-final echo-status sequence.
Default template (Conclave formal):
PRED (STATE/ACT)
(TOPIC) optional topicalized constituent
ARGUMENTS (role clitics)
(ADJUNCTS) (often INST-marked)
ECHO-STATUS operators
EVIDENTIAL (if not fused with echo-status)
Field clipped permits argument scrambling because roles are clitic-marked; however, the clause-final echo layer remains comparatively stable.
Orthography: Nûm ta=en ech.
IPA: /ˈnuːm tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: nûm align/cohere ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT
Free: “You are aligned (I witness it).”
Notes: Predicate-first; patient role explicit; no agent is stated (default agent = ambient/collective unless specified).
III.2 Constituent order and scrambling
Ech’ta’reen is predicate-initial as a default. Because clitics identify roles, ARG order is pragmatically driven:
early = topical/anchored in discourse
late = focused/new information
Orthography: Nûm na=ka ta=en ech.
IPA: /ˈnuːm nɑʔˈkɑ tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: nûm align na=ka 1=AGT ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT
Free: “I align you (witnessed).”
Notes: In Conclave formal, explicit agents are used sparingly; using na=ka increases responsibility/ownership.
Orthography: Nûm ta=en na=ka ech.
IPA: /ˈnuːm tɑʔˈɛn nɑʔˈkɑ ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: nûm align ta=en 2=PAT na=ka 1=AGT ech EVID.WIT
Free: “It is you I’m aligning (and yes, it’s me doing it).”
Notes: Late placement of the agent is interpreted as contrast/focus in field logs.
III.3 Topic and focus marking
Topic and focus are marked by optional enclitics.
=tra TOP (topic anchor; “as for…”)
=sha FOC (contrastive focus)
Orthography: Veyl=tra sekh ech.
IPA: /ˈvejlʔˈtɾɑ sɛx ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: veyl=tra boundary=TOP sekh THR ech EVID.WIT
Free: “As for the Veil, it’s in threshold state (witnessed).”
Notes: Topic-marking is common when speaking with non-Ech’ta’reen interlocutors who require explicit discourse framing.
Orthography: Nûm=sha ta=en ech.
IPA: /ˈnuːmʔʃɑ tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: nûm=sha align=FOC ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT
Free: “It’s alignment (specifically) that you’re undergoing (witnessed).”
III.4 Copula absence and predicate types
No dedicated copula is required. STATE roots serve as predicates. ENTITY roots can predicate as states without additional morphology.
Orthography: Threnn’vahl reen.
IPA: /ˈtɾɛnʔˈvɑl riːn/
IGT: threnn’vahl record.LCH reen EVID.VRF
Free: “The record is latched (verified across echoes).”
Notes: Translator arrays often output “It’s official.” This is a pragmatic inference.
III.5 Valency and implicit arguments
Ech’ta’reen frequently omit agents.
With no AGT, the clause is interpreted as ambient-causation or collective responsibility, depending on register.
Orthography: Vahl ta=en ech.
IPA: /ˈvɑl tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: vahl latch/stabilize ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT
Free: “You are latched/stabilized (witnessed).”
Notes: Without consent marking, this may imply coercion; see IV.3.
III.6 Questions
Questions are encoded by a clause-final interrogative particle and prosodic rise.
hë Q (polar interrogative) /hɛː/ in Conclave formal; /hɛ/ in field clipped.
WH roots (used in situ):
mën WH.PERS “who”
kô WH.THING “what”
râ WH.LOC “where”
sën WH.MANNER “how”
Orthography: Nûm ta=en ech hë?
IPA: /ˈnuːm tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ hɛː/
IGT: nûm align ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT hë Q
Free: “Are you aligned (as I witness it)?”
Orthography: Kesh mën=ka ta=en ech hë?
IPA: /kɛʃ mɛnʔˈkɑ tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ hɛː/
IGT: kesh cross mën=ka WH.PERS=AGT ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT hë Q
Free: “Who crossed you (witnessed)?”
Notes: WH roots do not front by default; fronting is a contact-induced calque in Anchor creole.
III.7 Negation and prohibition
Negation is primarily state inversion rather than propositional “not.”
vael- INV (misaligned/negated state)
vu NEG (clause particle used when inversion is not semantically available)
ta’qesh PROH/CAUT (strong prohibition / do-not-proceed)
Orthography: Vael’nûm ta=en ech.
IPA: /ˈvɑ.ɛlʔˈnuːm tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: vael-nûm INV-align ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT
Free: “You are misaligned / not aligned (witnessed).”
Orthography: Ta’qesh kesh ech.
IPA: /tɑʔˈqɛʃ kɛʃ ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: ta’qesh PROH kesh cross ech EVID.WIT
Free: “Do not cross (I witness the danger).”
Notes: In field logs this is often mistranslated as “Stop.” The intended meaning includes “crossing here harms the Veil/you.”
III.8 Subordination and clause chaining
Subordination is expressed through particles rather than embedding-heavy syntax.
ke COMP (complement/“that”)
rhel CONC (concessive “even if” as a clausal operator)
sën REL (relativizer; also WH.MANNER in context)
Orthography: Threnn’ul’esh ke nûm ta=en reen.
IPA: /ˈtɾɛnʔʊlʔˈɛʃ kɛ ˈnuːm tɑʔˈɛn riːn/
IGT: threnn’ul’esh record.GRANT ke COMP nûm align ta=en 2=PAT reen EVID.VRF
Free: “I grant record-access that you are aligned (verified).”
Notes: Used in Conclave formal when a verification is being entered into communal record.
Orthography: Veyl sekh së nûm reen.
IPA: /ˈvejl sɛx sɛn ˈnuːm riːn/
IGT: veyl boundary sekh THR së REL nûm align reen EVID.VRF
Free: “The threshold-veil that is aligned (verified).”
III.9 Imperatives
Imperatives are typically predicate-only, often with evidential omitted (because the act is prospective), plus optional consent markers.
Orthography: Uth’nûm=uth.
IPA: /ʔʊθʔˈnuːm ʔʊθ/
IGT: uth-nûm=uth SOFT-align=CONS
Free: “Align with permission (let us do this consensually).”
PART IV — Semantics & pragmatics
IV.1 Echo-status system (replacement of tense)
Ech’ta’reen grammaticize echo-status rather than tense. Echo-status specifies how an event/state relates to the speaker’s accessible echo set.
Core markers
ech EVID.WIT: directly witnessed in the current echo.
reen EVID.VRF: verified across multiple echoes (cross-validation).
qir LOOP: recurrence/looping; the event/state is in a repeating attractor.
ruun RET: return-pattern; the event/state is expected to recur or has recurred.
na’vyr CLP: collapse adjacency; references a state/event that has been collapsed into “never-was” in this echo (taboo-adjacent).
Stacking and scope
When stacked, the default order is:
LOOP / RET / CLP (structural echo operators)
WIT / VRF (evidential layer)
Orthography: Qir nûm ta=en ech.
IPA: /qir ˈnuːm tɑʔˈɛn ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: qir LOOP nûm align ta=en 2=PAT ech EVID.WIT
Free: “You keep becoming aligned again (looped; I witness it).”
Orthography: Ruun veyl’sekh reen.
IPA: /ruːn ˈvejlʔsɛx riːn/
IGT: ruun RET veyl-sekh boundary-THR reen EVID.VRF
Free: “The veil-threshold state returns (verified across echoes).”
IV.2 Evidentiality as social contract
Evidentiality is not optional in Conclave formal. Omitting it is interpreted as:
uncertainty,
refusal to commit,
or deliberate concealment.
Field clipped allows omission when safety requires brevity.
Orthography: Ta’qesh.
IPA: /tɑʔˈqɛʃ/
IGT: ta’qesh PROH
Free: “Stop. Do not proceed.”
Notes: Omission of evidential is licensed because the utterance functions as an alarm packet.
IV.3 Consent marking and coercion implicatures
Consent is grammaticized via =uth.
Predicate=uth frames the act/state as consent-governed.
Participant=uth indicates that participant has granted consent.
Absence of consent marking with coercion-salient predicates (vahl, stabilizing; certain kesh contexts) yields a coercive implicature.
Orthography: Vahl=uth ta=en=uth ech.
IPA: /ˈvɑl ʔʊθ tɑʔˈɛn ʔʊθ ɛt͡ʃ/
IGT: vahl=uth latch=CONS ta=en=uth 2=PAT=CONS ech EVID.WIT
Free: “You are being stabilized, with consent (witnessed).”
Notes: Field translators frequently collapse this to “I’m helping you,” losing the moral/legal force of consent marking.
IV.4 Truth, verification, and “cross-echo ethics”
Ech’ta’reen distinguish:
local truth (true in this echo, unverified)
cross-echo truth (reen-marked; verified across echoes)
The ethical system (keth) is encoded in lexical selection and evidential choice; stating reen without basis is a major breach.
IV.5 Taboo and sacred speech constraints
True-name taboo
True names are resonance chords, recorded in field notes as ⟦…⟧. Speaking a true name in foreign reality is avoided; reference strategies include:
role-only reference (AGT/PAT without lexical name)
echo-tags (event identifiers)
demonstratives and topic markers
Dead-name taboo
The dead are referenced by echo-tags or record states, not personal names. This produces a pragmatic pattern in condolence: record-latched rather than “gone.”
Collapse adjacency (na’vyr)
The CLP marker na’vyr is restricted to ritual register or formal Conclave audit contexts.
IV.6 Register system (predictable variation)
Conclave formal
conservative /ʔ/ boundary marking
mandatory evidential
full echo stacking
reduced pronoun usage
Field clipped
vowel shortening, occasional loss of length contrast
frequent standalone alarm packets (e.g., ta’qesh)
role clitics preserved; echo operators may be reduced to a single marker
Ritual register
increased /q/ usage; licensing of restricted lexemes
stronger [r], longer vowels
doubled boundary stops as delimiter
Anchor creole
Anchors (phase-locked) develop contact grammar:
increased pronoun frequency
optional calqued linear tense particles (treated as loan-operators)
adoption of Common lexemes for material culture (including ship life) as marked borrowings ‹…›
Crucially, creole elements are treated as contact-layer, not baseline Ech’ta’reen grammar.
